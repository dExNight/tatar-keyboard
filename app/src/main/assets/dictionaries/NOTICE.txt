Dictionary data notice

Both shipped dictionaries are derived from downloadable text corpora in the Leipzig
Corpora Collection.

Source catalogue: https://wortschatz.uni-leipzig.de/en/download
Terms: https://wortschatz.uni-leipzig.de/en/usage
License: Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International (CC BY 4.0)
https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/

Recommended citation:
D. Goldhahn, T. Eckart, U. Quasthoff, "Building Large Monolingual
Dictionaries at the Leipzig Corpora Collection: From 100 to 200 Languages",
LREC 2012.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
tatar_top100k_v1.tdict.zlib

Derived from the Tatar corpora tat_mixed_2015_1M, tat_news_2015_1M, and
tat_web_2018_1M.
Catalogue page: https://wortschatz.uni-leipzig.de/en/download/tat

Changes by the Tatar Keyboard project: the three word-frequency lists were
decoded, validated, normalized to NFC lowercase, filtered to the modern Tatar
Cyrillic alphabet and a 64-code-point limit, merged with summed frequencies,
ranked, cut to 100,000 forms, lexically reordered, packed into a versioned
binary format with SHA-256, and zlib-compressed.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
russian_top100k_v1.tdict.zlib

Derived from the Russian corpora rus_news_2022_1M, rus_news_2019_1M, and
rus_wikipedia_2021_1M.
Catalogue page: https://wortschatz.uni-leipzig.de/en/download/Russian

Changes by the Tatar Keyboard project: the three word-frequency lists were
decoded, validated, normalized to NFC lowercase, filtered to the 33-letter
Russian alphabet (with "e" and "yo" kept apart, and hyphenated and Latin forms
excluded) and a 64-code-point limit, merged with summed frequencies, ranked, cut
to 100,000 forms, lexically reordered, packed into the same versioned binary
format with SHA-256, and zlib-compressed.

Conversational word frequencies -- measured and queued, NOT yet packed

Two further collections were measured and turned into word-by-word acceptance
queues (docs/DICTIONARY-RU-CONV-REVIEW.tsv, docs/DICTIONARY-TT-CONV-REVIEW.tsv).
No word from either is inside the .tdict.zlib files above yet. They are named here
now because both collections ask to be named by anyone who uses them, and the work
that uses them is already done; when the words are merged, this section loses its
"not yet" and joins the derivation recorded for each asset above.

Tatoeba

Terms of use: https://tatoeba.org/eng/terms_of_use
Sentences are contributed by Tatoeba's members; CC BY 2.0 FR permits use,
modification and redistribution on the single condition that the source is cited.

OpenSubtitles, taken from the OPUS collection (OpenSubtitles v2024)
http://www.opensubtitles.org/
Recommended citation: P. Lison, J. Tiedemann, "OpenSubtitles2016: Extracting Large
Parallel Corpora from Movie and TV Subtitles", LREC 2016.
OpenSubtitles publishes NO license grant for this text -- not an unclear license, an
absent one. What the collection does ask is a link back to opensubtitles.org from
anything built on its data; that link is on the line above and on the app's "Data
sources" screen. The project's operator decided on 2026-08-24 to use the data
anyway, with the absence of a grant stated. docs/CORPUS-OS.md records the decision
and the measurements; docs/PUBLISH-CHECKLIST.md carries it to release as a known
risk rather than a settled question.

Changes by the Tatar Keyboard project (planned, same as for the sources above):
lines are deduplicated, tokens are normalized to NFC lowercase and filtered to the
language alphabet, proper nouns are removed by capitalization evidence, forms seen
fewer than three times are dropped, and only counts are kept -- with one stated
exception. The acceptance queues are read in portions (docs/review-batches/), and a
word that cannot be judged by its spelling alone carries one short example sentence
so the reviewer can judge it. Those sentences come from Tatoeba ONLY, which is what
CC BY 2.0 FR grants in exchange for the citation above. No OpenSubtitles sentence is
reproduced anywhere in this repository; words seen only there carry a list of their
neighbouring words with counts instead, which is a fact about the text and not the
text.
No sentence from either collection is stored in this repository or in the app.
